I've been interested in aerospace for a while, but explaining exactly why is harder than it sounds.

Part of it is the aircraft themselves. There is something fascinating about a machine that can lift hundreds of thousands of pounds into the air, travel faster than sound, or carry people and equipment across the world. The engineering behind that is impressive, but the more I learn about it, the more I realize how much is happening beneath the surface.

Aerospace brings together a lot of the subjects I enjoy: physics, mathematics, software, and hardware. An aircraft is not just a collection of mechanical parts. Its design involves aerodynamics, structures, propulsion, controls, materials, and increasingly, software. Changing one part of the system can affect everything else. I like that complexity.

I'm also interested in how much there is that I don't understand yet. I have spent time learning about computational fluid dynamics and experimenting with ways to predict aerodynamic behavior. Working on those problems has made aerospace feel less like a distant career and more like a field I can actually begin exploring now. It has also shown me how difficult engineering can be when the theory meets a real problem.

I don't know exactly what I want to do within aerospace. I could see myself working on aircraft, spacecraft, propulsion, flight systems, or something I haven't even encountered yet. I don't think I need to decide that right now.

For the moment, I want to keep learning the fundamentals, building things, and finding problems worth working on. Aerospace is a huge field, and that is part of what makes it exciting to me.

I don't just want to be interested in aircraft. I want to understand how they work, why they are designed the way they are, and how they could be made better.
