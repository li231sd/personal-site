Software tells you when it is unhappy. Hardware often just gets warm.

This board was supposed to be a quick afternoon project: read a sensor, filter the noisy signal, and print the result to a tiny display. Instead, the display stayed blank while every measurement looked almost reasonable.

The breakthrough was not a clever circuit. It was slowing down enough to check the physical story one connection at a time. The ground was present, but not where I thought it was. The signal had a shape, but the wrong reference. The code was innocent.

A soldering iron is a good reminder that debugging is not always about finding the bad line. Sometimes it is about rebuilding your picture of the system until the symptoms stop being surprising.
