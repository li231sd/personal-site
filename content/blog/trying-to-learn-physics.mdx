I've been trying to learn physics more seriously, and it has not exactly been going according to plan.

I knew it would be difficult. Physics involves mathematics, problem-solving, and a lot of concepts that take time to understand. Still, there is a difference between knowing something will be challenging and actually sitting down with a problem and realizing you have no idea where to start.

One of the biggest challenges has been figuring out what I don't know. I can follow an explanation and sometimes feel like I understand the concept. Then I try a problem on my own, and suddenly that understanding does not seem as solid as I thought it was. Recognizing which principles apply, translating a situation into equations, and knowing what assumptions are reasonable are all harder than simply following a worked example.

I also have to resist the temptation to rush. It is easy to jump ahead to interesting topics because I want to learn about aircraft, flight, and the physics behind them. But skipping fundamentals usually catches up with me. If I don't understand the basics, more advanced material becomes a collection of equations that I can imitate without really understanding.

That has been frustrating, especially when I have other things I want to work on. I would like to make progress quickly, but physics does not seem particularly interested in how quickly I want to learn it.

For now, I'm trying to focus on building a foundation instead of measuring progress by how advanced the material looks. I need to spend more time working through problems independently, identifying gaps in my understanding, and going back when something doesn't make sense.

I don't have a neat success story to write about yet. I'm still figuring it out, and some of it is not going very well.

Maybe that is worth documenting too. Learning something difficult is not always a steady progression. Sometimes it means getting stuck, realizing your approach isn't working, and trying again.
