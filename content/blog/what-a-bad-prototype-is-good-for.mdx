---
title: "What a Bad Prototype Is Good For"
description: "A postmortem on a prototype that failed its original test but answered a more important question about the product."
date: "2026-06-18"
tags: ["Prototyping", "Product", "Lessons"]
type: "Experiment"
---

The prototype did not work. That sentence sounds final until you ask what it was supposed to teach you.

Our first version made the happy path look convincing, but it collapsed as soon as the user brought their own context. The interface assumed too much, the defaults hid important choices, and the thing we called a shortcut was really just a missing explanation.

We kept the parts that made the central action feel fast and removed almost everything around them. The second prototype had fewer screens, fewer settings, and a much clearer failure state. It was less impressive in a demo and more useful in a real session.

A prototype earns its keep by making an assumption cheap to change. Once it has done that, it has already succeeded, even if the original version never ships.
