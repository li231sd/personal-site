A paper list is not a reading system. It is just a more organized form of guilt.

The system that has worked best for me has three stages: collect broadly, choose narrowly, and leave a useful trace. The first stage is allowed to be messy. The second needs a hard limit. The third is where the learning actually compounds.

For each paper I keep only three notes:

- What question is this paper trying to answer?
- What would make its answer believable?
- What should I look for next?

Those questions are deliberately more useful than a summary. They force me to notice the experiment, the assumptions, and the next connection instead of copying the abstract into a notebook.

The goal is not to finish every paper. It is to become better at choosing the next one.
