---
title: "Why Small Tools Win"
description: "A short argument for building focused tools that remove one recurring annoyance instead of trying to become an entire platform."
date: "2026-08-31"
tags: ["Software", "Design", "Tools"]
type: "Note"
---

The best tools I use are usually embarrassingly specific. They do one thing, make one decision easier, or remove one tiny piece of friction from a routine I already have.

That narrowness is not a limitation. It is what makes the tool legible. You can understand it in a minute, trust it in a day, and notice when it is missing.

I have started treating every new feature as a question: does this make the core loop more satisfying, or does it just make the product sound more complete? The answer is often less flattering than the roadmap.

Small tools also leave room for judgment. They support a person who is already thinking rather than trying to predict every possible workflow. That makes them easier to maintain, easier to explain, and much more likely to become part of someone's day.
